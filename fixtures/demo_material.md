# Evidence in Everyday Decisions

Good readers do more than understand individual words. They look for evidence, compare ideas, and decide whether a conclusion is supported by the text.

Mina wanted to improve her English vocabulary. She first tried memorizing a long list once a week, but she forgot most of the words. Then she changed her plan. Every day, she reviewed four words, wrote one sentence for each word, and checked the words again before bed. After two weeks, she could use more words in conversation.

The story does not prove that one study method works for everyone. However, it gives useful evidence: short and repeated practice helped Mina remember and apply vocabulary. A careful reader should separate this evidence from a broad claim.

## Reading strategy

1. Circle the claim.
2. Underline the evidence.
3. Mark contrast words such as *however* and *but*.
4. Explain the conclusion in your own words.

## Language focus

- evidence: information that supports an idea
- context: words and situations around an expression
- interpret: explain the meaning of something
- contrast: show how two ideas are different
- conclusion: a final judgment based on information
